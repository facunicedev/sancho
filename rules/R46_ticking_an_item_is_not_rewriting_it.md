# R45. Ticking a checklist item does not mean rewriting it

**Ticking an item is changing `[ ]` to `[x]`, and nothing else.** The result, the final figure and what you learned go in the task log, not into the item's wording.

The gate identifies the checklist by **the text of its items, with the boxes stripped out**: ticking what is done does not invalidate the signature, but changing what an item says does — and rightly so, because that is a different list from the one they signed.

## Where this came from

In one session the signature expired three times, and all three times it was self-inflicted: rewriting the wording while ticking it, so that "23 of 35 done" became "26 of 28 alive", and the gate saw a different list.

The cost was not asking for the signature again. **When the signature expires, writes from agents still running fall with it**: two research agents finished their work and could not save their record, so it had to be transcribed by hand from what they reported. A third lost the file it was writing to as it went, which is exactly what [R41](R41_write_as_you_go.md) exists to protect.

And before anything was changed, the hook was run: the checklist fingerprint comes out the same ticked and unticked, and different only when the text is touched. **The diagnosis that the gate was broken was false**, and defending it would have meant changing a hook that works — [R39](R39_the_cause_not_the_symptom.md) biting its own tail: before fixing the cause, you have to know what the cause is.

## If the wording really is wrong

Fix it, accept that the signature expires, and **ask for it again with the summary in front of them** ([R40](R40_summarise_what_you_ask_them_to_sign.md)). What you do not do is fix it while agents are running: collect their results first.
